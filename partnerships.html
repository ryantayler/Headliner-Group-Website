What happens when we partner

We take a minority share instead of a fee, then we go and build the partnerships your business should already have had. Here is how that works and who it works for.

Nobody is the best at all of it

The instinct in this industry is to pull everything in house. Your own AV, your own crew, your own venue if the numbers ever stack up. It feels like control and it reads like margin, and it quietly puts a ceiling on the business.

We think the ceiling comes off when a business partners with people who are genuinely the best at their piece, and we back that opinion with our own money and our own time.

Most event businesses try to do everything themselves. Their own AV, their own crew, their own venue if they can swing it. It protects the margin and caps everything else, because nobody is the best at all of it. I believe you grow faster by partnering with people who are the best at their piece. You give up a clip of the ticket. You get back quality, reputation, and the time to be great at the thing you’re actually great at.

Two lanes, and your goal decides the mix

Most of what we do is finding the right people and getting a deal done between you and them. The rest is going through the business itself and making it cheaper to run. Almost every partnership uses both, in different proportions.

LANE ONE

Growth through partnerships

We find the suppliers, the clients and the people worth knowing, then we negotiate the deal on your behalf. It usually takes one of four shapes.

REACH

A partnership that puts you in front of rooms you were never going to reach on your own.

MARGIN

Partnering into your own supply chain so you buy better and keep more. AV is the obvious one. Same price to your client, better price to you, and the difference is yours.

REVENUE, BY SERVICE

An extra service your clients already need and are already paying somebody else for.

REVENUE, BY AUDIENCE

Someone whose customers look like yours without competing for them. You both walk away with more clients than you had.

A production office mid week. Whiteboard, screens, someone working through a schedule.

LANE TWO

Efficiency and operations

The unglamorous half. We go through your processes, your products and the way work actually moves through the business, then we use what we know about this industry to take cost and friction out of it.

Quoting, scheduling, pipeline, handover, the reporting nobody reads. Usually the fastest money in the whole partnership, because it is money the business is already making and losing on the way out.

It starts with what you want

Before anything else we work out what you actually want out of this, because a founder chasing margin and a founder who wants their life back need different work done. What follows is the sequence when the answer is that you want to stop being the operator.

01

Processes

We write down how the work actually gets done, which is never quite how anyone thinks it gets done. This is the part most owners have carried in their head for fifteen years.

02

Systems

The processes go somewhere they run without you. Tools, templates and the rules for who does what when it goes sideways.

03

Team

Names against the parts. Some of them already work for you and are waiting to be asked. Some of them we go and find.

04

Build plan

What gets handed over, in what order, with what has to be true before each piece moves. Agreed with you before anything starts.

05

Execute

We run the plan with you, not at you. Your team hears it from you, and we are in the room for the parts that are hard.

06

Trial

You step out for somewhere between seven and thirty days and we find out what breaks. Nothing else in this list tells you the truth the way this does.

07

If it breaks, we go again

Back to whichever step it broke at, with what the trial taught us. More than one run is normal and we would rather tell you that here than discover it together later.

08

Sixty more days

Once it holds, we stay on for another sixty days while you get properly out. The business keeps running and you stop being the thing holding it up.

In an emergency a supplier says no when it’s inconvenient and a partner says yes to help you.

We get paid in equity

A consultant gets paid whether or not anything changes. We take a minority share in the business instead, so the only way this is worth anything to us is if the business is worth more than it was when we found it.

MOSTLY THIS

Minority share for the work

We do the work and take a minority share of the business rather than invoicing you for it. No retainer, no hourly rate, no report at the end.

SOMETIMES

Capital investment

Cash in for equity, where the business needs money as well as work. It is on the table and it is not the main game right now.

IF THAT IS WHAT YOU WANT

A full sale

If the honest answer is that you want out, we will look at buying the business outright and rolling it into the group.

How much and how long are both per deal, and we will not pretend otherwise. Anybody putting a percentage or a timeline in front of you before they have seen inside the business is guessing.

Who this works for

We are not fussy about what state you are in. We are fussy about knowing your world well enough to be useful in it from the first week.

THE BUSINESS

  • Turning over $1m or more, and that number is open to a conversation
  • Australian. We do not work internationally
  • Live event production, or close enough that we already know how it works
  • Already trading properly, so there is something real to grow

THE OWNER

  • Any situation. Growing, plateaued, or quietly done with it
  • Still hands on is good, because that is the owner we can get out
  • Clear on what they want personally, or willing to work it out with us
  • Honest about what is broken, because we will find it anyway
