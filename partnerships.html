What happens when we partner

If you’re not partnering, you’re doing it wrong

In live events there’s a one hundred percent chance something goes wrong. There’s simply too many moving parts for nothing to break, and the only real question is what it costs you when it does.

Two weeks out from a two thousand person summit in Sydney, twice as many people had registered as anyone had planned for. The room held a thousand. Turning the other thousand away wasn’t on the table, so the whole event moved into a bigger room, and then the venue came back and said it couldn’t deliver even the most basic AV in there on two weeks notice.

One call to an AV partner fixed it. They were running shows in other states. They’d never admit this, but it was genuinely inconvenient for them, and they said yes anyway and made it happen.

Shop around on every job and there’s nobody to make that call to. What those thousand people were worth is more than a business saves in years of squeezing quotes. That’s why we go and build the partnerships on your behalf instead of handing you a longer list of people to get quotes from.

“In an emergency a supplier says no when it’s inconvenient and a partner says yes to help you.”Ryan Tayler

It starts with what you want

Before anything else we work out what you actually want out of this, because a founder chasing margin and a founder who wants their life back need different work done. What follows is the sequence when the answer is that you want to stop being the operator.

01

Processes

We write down how the work actually gets done, which is never quite how anyone thinks it gets done. This is the part most owners have carried in their head for fifteen years.

02

Systems

The processes go somewhere they run without you. Tools, templates and the rules for who does what when it goes sideways.

03

Team

Names against the parts. Some of them already work for you and are waiting to be asked. Some of them we go and find.

04

Build plan

What gets handed over, in what order, with what has to be true before each piece moves. Agreed with you before anything starts.

05

Execute

We run the plan with you, not at you. Your team hears it from you, and we are in the room for the parts that are hard.

06

Trial

You step out for somewhere between seven and thirty days and we find out what breaks. Nothing else in this list tells you the truth the way this does.

07

If it breaks, we go again

Back to whichever step it broke at, with what the trial taught us. More than one run is normal and we would rather tell you that here than discover it together later.

08

Sixty more days

Once it holds, we stay on for another sixty days while you get properly out. The business keeps running and you stop being the thing holding it up.

Two ways we help you grow

One. Growth through partnerships

We find the suppliers, the clients and the people worth knowing, then we negotiate the deal on your behalf. Every partnership we build does at least one of four things, and the good ones do more than one.

The four pillars of partnership

Reach

Partnering with someone who already sells to the people you want to sell to. Same customers, no competition, and you get in front of a room you were never going to reach on your own.

Margin

Partnering into your own supply chain, up it and down it, so you spend less on what you were always going to buy. Your client pays what they always paid and the difference is yours.

Revenue

Partnering with people who deliver something your customers already need and are already buying somewhere else. Same clients, more of their spend, none of the cost of building it yourself.

Connection

Partnering with people for the doors they open. Not a service and not a discount. The introductions and the phone numbers you would have spent five years earning on your own.

Two. Growth through efficiency

Most businesses in this industry are already sitting on their next bit of growth. It is the same work they do now, done cleaner. Quoting, scheduling, handovers, how a job gets briefed and how it gets closed out.

Tighten those and the same team in the same week gets through more work at a better margin. It is the least exciting half of the job and it is usually the fastest money in the business, because it is money you are already making and losing again on the way out. Our efficiency matrix is what goes looking for it.

How we partner & invest

We are in the game of partnerships, so we partner with every business we work with. There are three ways that happens.

Minority & majority shares

We use the four pillars and our efficiency matrix to grow and streamline your business, and we take a share of it as your investment for that work. No retainer, no hourly rate, no report at the end.

Capital investment

If you need help and cash, or just cash, to grow the business, we are open to capital investment alongside the partnership.

Acquisitions

If you simply want out of your business, we are open to discussing a full acquisition and rolling it into the group.

This gives us a stake in everyone we partner with, which is what lets us be more invested in each partnership and a lot more picky about who we partner with. How much and how long are per deal, and anybody putting a percentage in front of you before they have seen inside the business is guessing.

Who this is for

Australian businesses in or around live events and production, turning over one to ten million, with an owner who wants the thing to grow, tighten up, or run without them. We are not fussy about which state you are in. We are fussy about knowing your world well enough to be useful in it from the first week.

$1m to $10m

Turning over between one and ten million. Under that there is usually not enough business yet for a partnership to be worth either side’s time. Over it and you want a different kind of partner to us.

Australia only

Every state and every capital, and the regional operators too. We do not work internationally, because the value we bring is a network and a network does not travel.

Live events & production

The industry itself and everything either side of it, up the supply chain and down it. If you supply this industry or you are supplied by it, you are close enough.

The owner matters more than the numbers

Growing, plateaued, or quietly done with the whole thing. All three are fine and all three need different work done, which is why the first conversation is about you rather than about the business.

Some owners are still on the tools and in every single job, and that is the owner we can get out. Some already have a manager running the day to day and want the next thing built on top of that. Both work. The one that does not work is an owner who will not say what is broken, because we find it in the first month anyway and we would rather hear it from you.
