Partnerships

What happens when we partner

No mystery, no process document. Here is what we look for, what we put in, and what the first ninety days look like.

The thesis

Specialists beat generalists, every time

Most event businesses try to do everything themselves. Their own AV, their own crew, their own venue if they can swing it. It protects the margin and caps everything else, because nobody is the best at all of it.

We believe you grow faster by partnering with people who are the best at their piece. You give up a clip of the ticket. You get back quality, reputation, and the time to be great at the thing you're actually great at.

That's why our goal is to be in business with, or partnered into, every area of live events and production in Australia.

Fit

What we look for

We're not looking for a turnaround and we're not looking for a startup. We're looking for a business that already works, run by someone who is tired of being the ceiling on it.

THE BUSINESS

  • Based in Australia, trading for three years or more
  • Revenue between $2m and $20m
  • Profitable, or one clear decision away from it
  • Real customers who come back, not one big contract holding it up
  • Somewhere in live events, production, or the supply chain around them

THE FOUNDER

  • Built it themselves and still cares how the job gets done
  • Wants the business to be bigger than they can take it alone
  • Honest about what's broken, because we'll find it anyway
  • Open to a partner in the room, not just a cheque in the account
  • Clear on what they want personally, whether that's growth or an exit

Value

What we actually bring

Money is the easy part and it's rarely the reason a good business is stuck.

01

Capital that isn't in a hurry

We buy in with our own money and we hold. There's no fund clock forcing a sale in three years.

02

The network

Crew, gear, venues, suppliers and clients built over more than 115 events. It becomes yours the day we shake hands.

03

Sales and growth

Pricing, pipeline, proposals and the follow up that most production businesses never get around to.

04

Systems and optimisation

The boring infrastructure. Quoting, scheduling, reporting, and knowing your real margin per job before you quote the next one.

05

New ventures

The product or revenue line you've been describing for years and never had the room to build.

06

The rest of the group

Every other business we're in becomes a supplier, a customer, or a referral source for yours.

Process

From first coffee to done

Roughly ninety days from a real conversation to a signed partnership, and you'll know where you stand at every step. If it isn't a fit we'll tell you at step two, not step five.

01

A conversation

A coffee or a call. What you've built, what's frustrating you, and what you actually want out of the next five years. Nothing signed, nothing shared.

02

A straight answer

Within a week we tell you whether this is a fit. If it isn't, you get the reason and any introductions we can make that would be more use to you.

03

Under the bonnet

Numbers, contracts, people, gear and customers. We do this ourselves rather than sending a team of graduates to interrogate your bookkeeper.

04

The shape of the deal

We put a written offer in front of you. What we're buying, what it's worth, what you keep, what changes, and what you're on the hook for afterwards.

05

Paperwork and handover

Lawyers do their bit. We plan the first ninety days together before anything is announced, so your team hears a plan rather than a rumour.

06

Then we go to work

Weekly for the first quarter, monthly after that. We're in the room for the decisions that matter and out of your way for the ones that don't.

In an emergency a supplier says no when it's inconvenient and a partner says yes to help you.

Structure

Four shapes this can take

Founders want different things and the deal should follow the person, not a template.

A

We come in beside you

We take a stake, you keep control and keep running it. Best when you love the business and just want it to move faster.

B

We take the larger share

You take real money off the table now, stay on running the business, and keep a share of what it becomes.

C

You exit, slowly

We buy the whole business and you stay for an agreed handover while we find and settle the person who replaces you.

D

You exit, cleanly

We buy the whole business and you're done. Your name stays on what you built and your people keep their jobs.

Straight up

What we don't do

Worth saying out loud so nobody wastes a meeting.

  • Strip a business for parts or gut the team on day one
  • Rename everything Headliner and bury what you built
  • Move in and run your job for you
  • Buy a business we don't understand
  • Flip it to someone else in three years
Crew pack down at the end of a show. The end of a job done properly.
