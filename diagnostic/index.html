One constraint. Not a to do list.

What it asks

It asks for facts, never for
your opinion of your own business

Nobody gets asked whether lead generation is a problem. An owner who believes only they can sell will tell you it isn’t. So every question asks for something you either know or you don’t, and the answer carries the benchmark with it.

Four answers to every question. Two good, two bad. No middle, so there is no fence to sit on, and no “not sure” either. What you don’t measure is a finding of its own, and it gets asked for directly.

    What comes back

    One finding, in the order you would act on it. The second half of every paragraph is your own answers, quoted back, so nothing in it is a guess about your business.

    You are supply constrained

    Due to fulfilment

    Double your customers tomorrow and you couldn’t deliver, because there isn’t enough capacity in the business. You’re running at or near full and you’re turning work away most months.

    A real verdict, out of a real answer set.

      Six constraints, two sides of one question

      Double your customers tomorrow. If that wouldn’t help you, you are supply constrained, because either you can’t deliver them or delivering them doesn’t pay. If it would, you are demand constrained, and the capacity is already sitting there.

      And eleven risks on the way past

      A constraint is what’s capping the business. A risk is what could end it. They are scored apart, because a business can be well run and still be one phone call from a problem.
