You are cash flow constrained

What it says

  • You're profitable on paper and short of cash in practice. The work is sold and the money hasn't landed, so there's nothing sitting there to fund the next job. Same for everyone
  • you get paid their Q7 answer Their answer dropped in
  • the balance lands their Q9 answer Their answer dropped in
  • you're owed their Q13 answer past its due date Their answer dropped in
  • their Q8 answer Their answer dropped in
  • you're holding their Q10 answer Their answer dropped in
  • That's a timing problem rather than a rate problem. Every job you win right now costs you money before it pays you, which is why winning more of them is currently the worst move available to you. Same for everyone

How to fix it  7 written, 4 conditional, at most 5 ever print

You need to close the gap between doing the work and getting paid for it. Every other fix in the business is waiting on this one.

  1. You take nothing upfront, which is this whole problem in one answer. Change that before anything else on this list. Only if Q7 is “Nothing upfront, invoiced on completion” or “Nothing upfront, invoiced on terms after”
  2. Move to a deposit on every job. Fifty percent thirty days out and the balance the week before, so the money is in before your costs go out. Same for everyone
  3. Line the deposit up to land before your cost of goods does. On jobs where the number moves on the day, charge the balance afterwards and refund the difference. Same for everyone
  4. Invoice within twenty four hours of the job finishing. Whatever the gap is between finishing and invoicing, that gap is yours to close. Same for everyone
  5. Collect what's already overdue before you chase anything new. It's the cheapest money available to you. Only if Q13 is “One to four weeks' revenue” or “More than a month's revenue”
  6. Put terms in writing before the next job starts, and chase on day one past due. Only if Q51 is “Month to month, cancellable anytime” or “Handshake and email”
  7. Get a facility in place while the business is still profitable on paper. Banks lend to businesses that don't look like they need it. Only if Q12 is “Yes, clearly”

Don’t do this yet

Until the cash timing is fixed, leave these alone.

  1. Don't chase bigger jobs. Bigger jobs mean bigger upfront costs and longer payment cycles. Same for everyone
  2. Don't hire. Wages are the least flexible cost you can add to a cash problem. Same for everyone
  3. Don't buy equipment outright. Finance it, even if the interest annoys you. Same for everyone
  4. Don't discount to get paid faster. That's borrowing at a rate you'd never accept from a bank. Same for everyone
  5. Don't increase marketing spend. More demand you can't fund makes the hole deeper. Same for everyone

How to fix it  5 written, 0 conditional, at most 5 ever print

Hire one role that owns a layer of the business, with the authority to run it. Not another pair of hands underneath you.

  1. Write down every task you did last week. Same for everyone
  2. Against each one, write the role that should own it. A role, not a person already in the business. Same for everyone
  3. Whichever role has the most tasks against it, or the most hours, hire that role first. Same for everyone
  4. Give them responsibility and the authority to go with it. One without the other doesn't work. Same for everyone
  5. Train them yourself, then give them ninety days to prove they're the one. Same for everyone

How to fix it  5 written, 1 conditional, at most 5 ever print

You need to find out why customers leave or shrink, and fix that, before you spend another dollar finding new ones.

  1. Ring ten customers who stopped buying or cut back, and ask them straight. Not a survey. A phone call. Same for everyone
  2. Ask your biggest customers what they give to somebody else, and why. That work already exists, you're just not getting it. Only if Q26 is “About half” or “Less than half” or “A small slice, they spread it around”
  3. Look hard at what happens in the first thirty days after somebody buys. That's the part of the experience you control most tightly. Same for everyone
  4. Build one reason to come back that you initiate. A check in, or the next step offered before they go looking. Same for everyone
  5. Measure retention monthly, so a drop shows up while you can still do something about it. Same for everyone

Don’t do this yet

  2. Don't raise prices. Value perception is already the problem. Same for everyone
  3. Don't add a new product for existing customers who aren't buying the current one. Same for everyone
  4. Don't expand into a new market. You'd take the retention problem with you. Same for everyone
  5. Don't run a loyalty programme. A discount doesn't fix a value gap, it confirms one. Same for everyone

  • You don't fix this by dropping the price. You fix it by putting things into the offer that cost you very little and are worth real money to the buyer, and by removing whatever a prospect has to get over before they can say yes. Same for everyone

How to fix it  6 written, 2 conditional, at most 5 ever print

You need more value in the offer than the price is asking for, and the hurdles taken out of the way of somebody saying yes.

  1. List everything you already do for a customer that isn't written into the offer. That's value nobody can see. Same for everyone
  2. Add two things that cost you close to nothing and are worth real money to the buyer. A guarantee, or something they would otherwise have to go and source themselves. Same for everyone
  3. Write down every question a prospect asks after they get the quote, then answer all of them inside the quote itself. Only if Q33 is “Often” or “On most quotes”
  4. Put a price or a range where they can see it. Making somebody wait for a number is a hurdle before they have even decided. Only if Q35 is “Every job gets quoted from scratch” or “It depends who's asking”
  5. Sort your last twenty losses by reason and count them. If price keeps coming up, look at the value in the offer before you look at the number. Same for everyone
  6. Test the new offer on the next ten enquiries before you change anything else. Same for everyone

Don’t do this yet

Until the offer lands, leave these alone.

  1. Don't buy more leads. You'd pay more money for the same conversion rate. Same for everyone
  2. Don't hire a salesperson. They'd fail against the same offer you're failing against. Same for everyone
  3. Don't cut your price. Price is rarely the real reason, and you can't test it while the offer is unclear. Same for everyone
  4. Don't add more options. More choice is part of why these deals are stalling. Same for everyone
  5. Don't rebrand. The words are the problem, not the logo. Same for everyone

You are demand constrained

What it says

  • You've got capacity sitting idle and not enough people asking. Same for everyone
  • enquiries run their Q36 answer Their answer dropped in
  • volume has their Q38 answer over the last year Their answer dropped in
  • their Q40 answer Their answer dropped in
  • You could take on more work tomorrow, which is what separates this from a delivery problem. Nothing downstream is broken. The business just isn't being fed, and it won't start feeding itself. Same for everyone

How to fix it  6 written, 2 conditional, at most 5 ever print

You need one lead source running properly and consistently before you go anywhere near a second.

  1. Whatever you're doing to generate enquiries isn't running consistently. Start with the one thing you can do every week without fail. Only if Q40 is “On and off” or “No, it all comes to us”
  2. Pick the lead source that already brings your best customers and put real weight behind it. Same for everyone
  3. Go and ask for work. Outbound to the twenty businesses you'd most like to work with beats waiting on referrals. Same for everyone
  4. Everything arrives through one lead source. Stand up a second before that one changes. Only if Q48 is “One channel is nearly all of it”
  5. Ask every happy customer for one introduction. Same for everyone
  6. Give a lead source ninety days before you judge it. Anything shorter is guesswork. Same for everyone

Don’t do this yet

Until enquiries lift, leave these alone.

  1. Don't hire. You'd be adding capacity to capacity you already can't fill. Same for everyone
  2. Don't build a new product. The one you have hasn't been shown to enough people. Same for everyone
  3. Don't cut prices to stimulate demand. It rarely moves volume, and it permanently resets what customers expect to pay. Same for everyone
  4. Don't spread across five channels at once. One channel run properly beats five run badly. Same for everyone
  5. Don't wait for referrals to pick back up. Referral volume follows the number of customers you have, and that's the number that's short. Same for everyone

How to fix it  5 written, 2 conditional, at most 5 ever print

You need to know what each job actually earns you before you change anything. A price rise can cost you customers as easily as it can fix the margin.

  1. Raise prices on new work now. Ten percent across the board, and watch what actually happens. Only if Q43 is “1 to 2 years ago” or “More than 2 years ago” or “Never”
  2. Work out what each job really costs, your own time included, then rank your last twenty by what was left. Same for everyone
  3. Quote a contingency into the jobs that historically run over. You already know which ones they are. Only if Q44 is “Often” or “On most jobs”
  4. Find your worst three customers by margin, then reprice them or let them go. Same for everyone
  5. Don't discount to win. A discount is the fastest way to buy work you'll resent delivering. Same for everyone

Don’t do this yet

Until the margin moves, leave these alone.

  1. Don't chase volume. More work at this margin is more risk for the same money. Same for everyone
  2. Don't hire. Every head you add at this margin needs a lot of revenue standing behind it. Same for everyone
  3. Don't take the big thin job because it's good for the brand. Reputation doesn't pay wages. Same for everyone
  4. Don't discount anything. There's nothing left to give away. Same for everyone
  5. Don't invest in growth. Growing an unprofitable model makes it unprofitable faster. Same for everyone
