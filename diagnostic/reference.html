The twelve risks

Loudest first, top 4 print, bar is 55

A risk has to fire on an answer that is genuinely unusual, never on the answer most small businesses would give. That is why fragility was removed and why some answers below add weight without ever printing on their own.

One of something that should be several

You have a Key asset risk

Concentration

One piece of equipment or one vehicle is doing work you have no backup for. A breakdown or a long repair stops revenue you've already sold, and hire rates at short notice are set by people who know you're stuck.

Prints when they answer

  • 100 Q50 “named as the one that would hurt most”
  • 75 Q49 “One key piece of equipment, or one vehicle”

What to do

  1. Find out today what a replacement costs at short notice, and who actually has one.
  2. Put it on a service schedule and hold to it. Most failures give some warning.

You have a Key channel risk

Concentration

Your customers arrive answer to Q48. Channels change without asking you first, and an algorithm shift or one referrer going quiet can halve your enquiries inside a month.

If they answered Not sure. You weren't sure where your customers actually come from, which usually means one channel is doing more of the work than you think. A channel can change without asking you first, and one referrer going quiet can halve your enquiries inside a month.

Prints when they answer

  • 95 Q48 “One channel is nearly all of it”

Adds weight but never prints on its own

  • 50 Q48 “Two channels carry it”
  • 50 Q48 “Not sure”

What to do

  1. Stand up a second channel before you need it, and give it ninety days before you judge it.
  2. Write down what you'd do if the current one halved tomorrow. That plan is worth having on paper before you need it.

You have a Key client risk

Concentration

One customer carries answer to Q46 of your revenue. That customer can leave, or get bought by someone who already has their own suppliers, and neither is something you get a say in.

Prints when they answer

  • 100 Q46 “More than 50%”
  • 90 Q46 “30% to 50%”
  • 90 Q51 “Handshake and email” only when Q46 is c or d or e
  • 70 Q51 “Month to month, cancellable anytime” only when Q46 is c or d or e
  • 65 Q46 “20% to 30%”
  • 60 Q51 “Not sure” only when Q46 is c or d or e

Adds weight but never prints on its own

  • 35 Q46 “10% to 20%”

What to do

  1. Set a ceiling, say twenty percent of revenue from any one customer, and treat crossing it as the trigger to go and win two more.
  2. Get them onto a term agreement with a notice period, so a decision to leave gives you time to react.
  3. If the work is committed on a handshake, that is the first conversation to have, before the ceiling.

You have a Key employee risk

Concentration

One person knows how the work actually gets done, and very little of it is written down anywhere else. If they leave, the knowledge goes with them, and you'll be rebuilding it from scratch while still trying to deliver.

Prints when they answer

  • 100 Q50 “named as the one that would hurt most”

  • 85 Q52 “Almost none, it lives in people's heads”

Adds weight but never prints on its own

  • 50 Q52 “Not sure”
  • 45 Q52 “About half”

What to do

  2. Put a second person alongside them on the next three jobs, doing rather than watching.
  3. Record the five things that go wrong most often as one page checklists. People use checklists, they don't use manuals.

You have a Key geography risk

Concentration

The business sells into one city or region. A local downturn or a big competitor arriving hits all of your revenue at once rather than part of it.

Prints when they answer

  • 100 Q50 “named as the one that would hurt most”
  • 70 Q49 “The city or region you sell into”

What to do

  1. Take one thing you do well and sell it somewhere else, even at a small scale.
  2. Track what share of revenue comes from outside the home patch, and set a target for it.

You have a Key licence risk

You have a Key supplier risk

Concentration

You've got one supplier with no ready replacement. Their price rise is your price rise, and their bad year is your bad year.
