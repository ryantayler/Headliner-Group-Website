## Round 1, answered

### 1. Who the site is for

**Primary.** His exact ICP, owners of live events and production businesses. The site
wants them to engage. It also works as proof and status, so it has to look like a real
group before it is a big one.

**Secondary.** Event and production managers. They come for Free Sh!t, they go into the
database, and that database is leverage. They are not expected to partner.

So the two audiences split cleanly by page. Home and Partnerships talk to owners.
Free Sh!t talks to managers. Nothing else has to serve both.

### 2. What can be named

The group is currently **Headliner Group and BusinessOS**. That is it.

### 3. How many businesses

Not six. The stat band on `ryan.html` says six and it is wrong. See the open question
about what the page says with a group this size.

### 4. The offer

Four things, all built on the same idea. Headliner takes a **minority share in exchange
for services rather than cash**, and pays for it with access to the partner network.

1. **Margin through partnerships.** Connect the business to the partner group so it buys
   better. The worked example is AV. Cheaper AV, same sell price, higher margin.
2. **Scale through partnerships.** Minority share, then build child companies or new
   branches of the business using the partnership method.
3. **Optimisation.** Sales pipeline, process and systems work on the existing business.
4. **Access.** The database and the partnerships come with the deal.

Also on the table, and worth stating on the site even though neither is the current focus:

- **Capital investment.** Cash for equity. Possible later, not the play right now.
- **Full acquisition.** If an owner wants to sell, Headliner buys the business and rolls
  it into the group.

### Voice, amended by Ryan

**Headliner Group is a different brand to Ryan Tayler.** Equity, minority share,
acquisition, roll up and the rest of that language are **allowed on Headliner pages**,
written in his voice. They stay **off `ryan.html`** entirely.

This overrides the blanket ban in the `ryan-voice` skill, which is now read as applying
to his personal brand. Every other voice rule still holds everywhere: no dashes in prose,
no colons outside lists, Australian spelling, never framed as an operator, and the proof
numbers stay 115+ live events and 56,000+ attendees.
